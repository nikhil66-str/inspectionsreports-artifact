Common Reporting Mistakes to Avoid

Learn from these frequently seen errors that create liability exposure for chimney inspectors.

❌ Mistake #1: Making Absolute Safety Claims

What inspectors say: "This chimney is 100% safe" or "You're good to go - no problems here"

Why it's problematic: No inspection can guarantee future safety. Conditions change over time.

What to say instead: "No deficiencies were observed during this Level 2 inspection. Continued annual inspections per NFPA 211 are recommended."

❌ Mistake #2: Overstating Scope

What inspectors say: "We checked everything" or "Complete inspection performed"

Why it's problematic: Level 2 inspections have specific boundaries; concealed areas exist.

What to say instead: "Inspection performed per NFPA 211 Level 2 standards. Readily accessible and visible components were examined. Concealed conditions are beyond scope."

❌ Mistake #3: Prescribing Specific Solutions

What inspectors say: "You need to install a 6-inch stainless liner" or "Replace the entire chimney"

Why it's problematic: Inspectors observe and recommend; contractors diagnose and bid solutions.

What to say instead: "Recommend evaluation by a qualified professional to determine appropriate repair options."

❌ Mistake #4: Using Scare Tactics

What inspectors say: "This will burn your house down" or "Your family is in danger"

Why it's problematic: Emotional language can be seen as coercive; creates unnecessary panic.

What to say instead: "This condition does not meet NFPA 211 safety standards. Recommend immediate evaluation and do not operate until corrected."

❌ Mistake #5: Vague Observations

What inspectors say: "Some damage was noted" or "A few cracks were seen"

Why it's problematic: Lacks specificity needed for follow-up; creates ambiguity.

What to say instead: "A vertical crack approximately 8 inches in length was observed in the clay tile liner at 12 feet above the smoke shelf."

❌ Mistake #6: Offering Guarantees

What inspectors say: "Once this is fixed, you'll never have problems" or "This repair will last 20 years"

Why it's problematic: Creates warranties and contractual obligations you can't control.

What to say instead: "Correction of noted deficiencies will bring the system into compliance with current NFPA 211 standards."

❌ Mistake #7: Making Specific Referrals

What inspectors say: "Call Joe's Masonry to fix this - mention my name"

Why it's problematic: Creates conflict of interest appearance; liability if referred work is poor.

What to say instead: "Recommend repair by a CSIA-certified professional or qualified masonry contractor."

Self-Check Questions

  • Did I reference NFPA 211 standards where applicable?
  • Did I avoid absolute statements and guarantees?
  • Are my observations specific with measurements and locations?
  • Did I clearly state limitations and scope boundaries?
  • Are my recommendations professional and non-emotional?
  • Did I avoid prescribing specific solutions beyond my role?
