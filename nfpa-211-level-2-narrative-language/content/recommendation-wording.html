Recommendation Wording Best Practices

How you phrase recommendations affects both client understanding and your professional liability.

Urgency Levels

"Do not operate until corrected" or "Recommend immediate evaluation by a qualified professional"
"Recommend correction prior to further use" or "Should be addressed before the heating season"
"Recommend monitoring during annual inspections" or "Continue routine maintenance"

Phrasing Recommendations Safely

Situation Safe Recommendation Language Avoid
Cracked liner "Recommend evaluation by a qualified mason or CSIA-certified professional to determine repair options" "You need a new liner" (diagnostic conclusion)
Heavy creosote "Professional cleaning recommended per NFPA 211 guidelines due to creosote thickness exceeding 1/8 inch" "This will cause a chimney fire" (prediction)
Clearance violation "Clearance to combustibles does not meet NFPA 211 requirements. Recommend correction or installation of approved heat shielding" "This is illegal and dangerous" (legal opinion)
Uncertain condition "Further evaluation recommended to determine the extent of the condition observed" "I'm not sure what this is" (unprofessional)
Beyond scope "Destructive testing would be required to evaluate this condition; recommend Level 3 inspection if serious hazard is suspected" "We can't see that" (incomplete)

Recommendation Structure

  1. State the observation: "A crack approximately X inches was observed at [location]"
  2. Reference the standard: "NFPA 211 requires..." or "This condition does not meet NFPA 211 requirements"
  3. Provide the recommendation: "Recommend evaluation/correction/repair by [qualified party]"
  4. Set the timeline: "Prior to use" / "Before next season" / "During next annual inspection"

Critical Reminder

Inspectors observe and recommend; they do not diagnose or prescribe specific solutions. Always recommend further evaluation by appropriate specialists when conditions exceed your scope or expertise.
