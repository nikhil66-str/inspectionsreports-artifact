Safe vs. Risky Language Examples

Understanding the difference between safe, defensible language and risky language is essential for professional protection.

Scope & Limitations

❌ Risky Language ✓ Safe Language Why It Matters
"We inspected everything" "Readily accessible and visible components were inspected in accordance with NFPA 211 Level 2 standards" Defines scope clearly and references standards
"This chimney is perfectly safe" "No deficiencies were observed during this Level 2 inspection" Avoids absolute guarantees; states what was observed
"The entire system was examined" "All accessible areas were examined. Concealed conditions are beyond the scope of this inspection" Acknowledges limitations explicitly

Findings & Conditions

❌ Risky Language ✓ Safe Language Why It Matters
"The flue is damaged" "A crack approximately 4 inches in length was observed in the clay tile liner at the smoke chamber transition" Specific, measurable, and localized observation
"This is a fire hazard" "Creosote deposits exceeding 1/8 inch were observed. NFPA 211 recommends cleaning when deposits reach this thickness" States observation and references standard, not opinion
"Everything looks great" "The chimney structure, liner, and clearances appeared to meet NFPA 211 requirements at the time of inspection" Uses qualified language and time-stamps observation

Recommendations

❌ Risky Language ✓ Safe Language Why It Matters
"You must repair this immediately or your house will burn down" "Recommend evaluation and repair by a qualified mason prior to further use" Avoids scare tactics; provides professional recommendation
"Once you fix this, you'll be fine" "Correction of noted deficiencies will bring the system into compliance with current NFPA 211 standards" No guarantee of future safety
"Call Bob's Chimney - they can fix it" "Recommend repair by a CSIA-certified professional or qualified masonry contractor" Avoids specific referrals; states qualifications needed

Key Principle

Report what you observed, not what you assume. Use specific measurements, locations, and reference standards. Avoid absolute statements, guarantees, and subjective judgments.
