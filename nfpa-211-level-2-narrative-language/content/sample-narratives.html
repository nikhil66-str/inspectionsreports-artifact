Sample Narrative Examples

Complete narrative examples for common Level 2 inspection scenarios.

Scenario 1: Property Transfer - No Deficiencies

Inspection Trigger:

"This Level 2 inspection was performed in accordance with NFPA 211 due to a change in property ownership. NFPA 211 requires a Level 2 inspection when a property is transferred, when an appliance is changed, or when damage is suspected following an external event."

Summary Finding:

"The chimney system serving the masonry fireplace was inspected in accordance with NFPA 211 Level 2 standards. A video scan of the flue interior was performed, and accessible portions of the attic, basement, and exterior were examined. No deficiencies requiring immediate attention were observed at the time of inspection. Minor creosote deposits were noted; cleaning is recommended prior to the next heating season."

Recommendation:

"Continue annual Level 1 inspections per NFPA 211. Schedule cleaning to remove light creosote deposits before regular use."

Scenario 2: Property Transfer - Minor Deficiency Found

Inspection Trigger:

"This Level 2 inspection was required under NFPA 211 due to a change in ownership and real estate transfer."

Summary Finding:

"A Level 2 video scan inspection of the masonry chimney revealed a crack approximately 6 inches in length in the clay tile liner at the smoke chamber transition. The crack appears to be a separation at the mortar joint rather than a fracture through the tile itself. No other deficiencies were observed in accessible areas. Clearances to combustible materials in the attic and basement meet NFPA 211 requirements."

Recommendation:

"Recommend evaluation by a CSIA-certified chimney professional or qualified masonry contractor to determine if repair or relining is necessary. The system should not be used until evaluated and any necessary corrections are completed."

Scenario 3: Post-Modification - Deficiency Identified

Inspection Trigger:

"This Level 2 inspection was performed following the installation of a new wood stove insert. NFPA 211 requires a Level 2 inspection whenever an appliance is changed or added to an existing chimney system."

Summary Finding:

"Video scan examination of the flue interior revealed significant creosote deposits exceeding 1/4 inch thickness in the upper flue section. Additionally, the existing clay tile liner shows evidence of thermal stress cracking. The newly installed appliance connector clearances to the combustible mantel do not meet NFPA 211 requirements (measured 14 inches; 18 inches required for unprotected combustibles)."

Recommendation:

"1. Do not operate the appliance until the following corrections are completed:
   • Increase clearance to combustibles or install approved heat shielding per NFPA 211
   • Remove creosote deposits through professional cleaning
   • Have a qualified professional evaluate the liner for potential relining
2. Consider stainless steel liner installation for optimal safety and performance with the new appliance"

Limitation Language (Standard for All Scenarios)

"This inspection is limited to readily accessible and visible components. Concealed areas, buried foundations, and conditions requiring destructive testing are beyond the scope of this Level 2 inspection and would require a Level 3 inspection per NFPA 211 if serious hazards are suspected."
