Common Inspection Report Failures

The issues below reflect common documentation problems reported by homeowners, insurers, and real estate professionals. These failures are typically related to reporting and communication, not the quality of the inspection itself.

  • Inspection completed, but report never delivered.
    Homeowners receive an invoice but no written inspection findings, leaving no usable record for insurance or future reference.
  • Report language is vague or informal.
    Findings are described casually or unclearly, making it difficult for homeowners or third parties to understand the actual condition of the system.
  • Photos taken but not attached to the report.
    Visual evidence exists on a technician’s phone but never makes it into the final documentation.
  • Invoice sent instead of inspection report.
    Clients receive proof of payment, but no inspection details, creating confusion and frustration.
  • Verbal explanation not reflected in writing.
    What was explained on-site is not captured accurately in the report, leading to misunderstandings later.
  • Unclear inspection scope.
    Reports fail to specify what was visually inspected versus what was not accessible, increasing liability and mistrust.
  • Delayed or repeated follow-ups.
    Clients must contact the office multiple times to request reports, corrections, or missing documentation.

Clear, timely, and well-structured documentation helps prevent these issues and protects both the homeowner and the inspection professional.
