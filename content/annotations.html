Report Annotations & Notes

The notes below explain why each section of this example report exists and what it is intended to communicate. These annotations highlight common misunderstandings and documentation gaps observed in real-world inspections.

Inspector & Company Information

Clearly identifying the inspecting company and individual inspector helps establish accountability and credibility. Insurance providers and real estate professionals often require this information to verify who performed the inspection and when it occurred.

Inspection Scope Statement

Defining the inspection as a Level 1 visual inspection is critical. This clarifies that the inspection was limited to readily accessible areas and did not include concealed spaces, internal video scanning, or dismantling.

Reports that omit a clear scope statement often lead to confusion or unrealistic expectations about what was evaluated.

Visual Inspection Checklist

The checklist documents what was visually evaluated at the time of inspection and distinguishes between items that were observed versus those that were inaccessible or not applicable.

Explicitly marking items as Not Observed helps prevent assumptions that concealed areas were inspected.

Photo Documentation

Photographs provide visual context and support written findings. Including captions that describe what each image represents helps homeowners and third parties understand the relevance of the photos.

Photos should reflect only what was visible at the time of inspection and should not imply evaluation of concealed conditions.

Findings vs. Recommendations

Separating observed findings from recommendations helps maintain clarity. Findings describe what was seen, while recommendations suggest next steps based on professional judgment.

Combining these sections can lead to misunderstandings or the perception of unnecessary upselling.

Plain-Language Summary

The summary is written for homeowners and non-technical readers. Its purpose is to explain the overall condition of the system in clear terms without technical jargon.

This section often becomes the most referenced part of the report and should accurately reflect the findings documented above.

Signature & Acknowledgment

The inspector acknowledgment confirms when the report was completed and reinforces that the inspection reflects conditions observed at that time only.

This section is not a certification or warranty, but a professional record of the inspection performed.
